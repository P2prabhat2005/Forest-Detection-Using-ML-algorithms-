# Technical input-data feasibility: Greece + British Columbia

**Checked 2026-10-11 UTC; scope:** two GFS run dates (2026-10-09 and 2026-10-10), one 1° × 1° box in each region. This is an input-data spot check, **not** a forecast-contract or label-compatibility pass. The label gate remains **FAIL**.

## Directly verified

- NOAA NOMADS GFS 0.25° GRIB2 filter returned HTTP 200 and valid `GRIB` payloads for all four date–region requests. Each selected box yielded a 5 × 5 grid; responses were only 1,390–1,446 bytes. The illustrative cycle was `00 UTC`, lead `f006` (valid 06 UTC), **not** an agreed operational issue time or complete next-day interval.
- Decoded GRIB parameter/level metadata and simple-packed values: 2 m temperature (`TMP`, K; convert °C = K − 273.15), 2 m relative humidity (`RH`, %), 10 m U/V wind (`UGRD`/`VGRD`, m s⁻¹; speed = √(U²+V²)), and surface total precipitation (`APCP`, kg m⁻² = mm water equivalent). All four responses contained these fields and 25 gridpoint values per field; no missing bitmap was declared. See the sample manifest for decoded ranges.
- `APCP` uses GRIB product template 4.8 (statistical accumulation), with an end time of 06 UTC and a six-hour interval in the sampled `f006` messages: **00–06 UTC**, not instantaneous precipitation. Two apparently duplicate `APCP` messages appeared in each filtered response; a production decoder must select/de-duplicate by complete GRIB metadata, not sum both. Later forecast leads must be inspected separately before assembling a next-day precipitation total.
- Source/run provenance can be recorded using URL, run/cycle/lead, box, GRIB centre/table identifiers, parameter/level/template/time range, retrieval time, byte count, and missingness. The manifest records these. NOAA's [NOMADS filter](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/gribfilter.php?ds=gfs_0p25) and [GFS product inventory](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/products/gfs/) support the product identity; [NCEP GRIB tables](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/docs/grib2/grib2_doc/grib2_table4-2-0-1.shtml) define the units.

## Not yet verified / contract decisions

1. **Cutoff and validity: NOT VERIFIED.** The existing proposed contract leaves local reporting-day boundaries and issue time open. A successful retrospective download does not establish when the forecast was first published. Need an agreed UTC issue/cutoff for each region, first-availability logs or timestamped archived-object metadata, and checks of every lead spanning the next local day. GFS runs four times daily, but cycle time is **not** publication time. Historical GFS model/version changes must be pinned for fair retrospective training. [NCEI GFS archive description](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/weather-climate-models/global-forecast).
2. **Static summaries: source feasibility only, NOT empirically derived here.** [Copernicus DEM GLO-30](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM) offers globally comparable elevation; [ESA WorldCover 2021 v200](https://esa-worldcover.org/en/data-access) offers global 10 m land cover. Both cover the two regions in principle, but no tiles were sampled and no ~25 km cell summary was computed. Choose a fixed equal-area projection/grid origin, clip source pixels, and record area-weighted elevation statistics, land-cover fractions, land/water fraction, no-data fraction, product version and tile IDs. A 2021 land-cover map is **not** automatically valid for earlier issue dates or as a current 2026 fuel map.
3. **Operational missingness and reproducibility: schema feasible, not proven.** Four successful near-term requests do not quantify outage/latency, cloud-independent static coverage, historical forecast retention, provider revision, or long-run missingness. Save immutable forecast bytes or source checksums and retrieval timestamps for actual experiments; the short-lived NOMADS endpoint alone is not a durable versioned archive.

**Decision:** weather variable retrieval/decoding is technically feasible in both regions; the *forecast-at-issue-time* claim and common static-grid summaries are not yet demonstrated. Do not mark the global gate PASS.

**Next action:** after the agencies settle reporting-day calendars, freeze a UTC issue/cutoff and next-day valid interval for both regions; then replay the full lead sequence on these same two dates from timestamped forecast archives and compute one common-grid DEM/land-cover sample with quality masks.
